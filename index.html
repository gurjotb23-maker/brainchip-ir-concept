BrainChip can't tell you how much it earned.

A $341M company. A retail-dominated register. And an investor deck that shows you a $163B market opportunity but won't tell you a single dollar of revenue.

Background

Who is BrainChip?

BrainChip Holdings (ASX: BRN) makes neuromorphic AI chips — specifically the Akida processor, designed for ultra-low-power edge AI inference. Partners include ARM, Mercedes-Benz, NASA and Nvidia. It's one of Australia's most retail-heavy tech stocks, with ~95% of its register held by retail investors. Market cap: ~A$341M.

The Diagnosis

What's broken

01

The deck is a marketing pitch, not an investor update

Every quarterly update leads with TAM charts ($163B, $84B market sizes), a logo salad of partners, and aspirational language. There is no revenue disclosure, no named paying customers vs research partners, no unit shipment numbers, no cash runway in plain English. Retail holders cannot answer the most basic question: is this business growing?

02

The capital raise contradiction

In November 2025, BrainChip raised A$35M from institutions at A$0.175 — a 10.3% discount. Weeks later, retail shareholders were offered a Share Purchase Plan capped at A$2M at a near 52-week low. The SPP closed at A$2.976M. Then a US$25M raise followed in December. Retail got the worst terms, the least capital, and no explanation of why the raises were structured this way.

03

Shareholders said it out loud and nothing changed

At the 2025 AGM, shareholders voted 76.6% against the remuneration report — the third consecutive strike. Attendees explicitly named communication, executive compensation, and share price performance as concerns. The company's response was silence. No town hall. No plain-English letter to holders. No acknowledgment.

BrainChip's problem isn't that they say nothing. It's that they say a lot — to the wrong audience, in the wrong format, about the wrong things.

The Fix

What I'd do about it

One page. Every quarter. No excuses.

The fix isn't a rebrand or a new agency. It's a Retail Shareholder Scorecard — a one-page plain-English operating update that BrainChip should publish every quarter alongside its ASX lodgement. No jargon, no TAM charts, no logo salad. Just the four things every retail holder needs to know: how much cash is left, whether revenue is moving, what the pipeline actually looks like, and what's coming next.

Process

AI Log

How I used AI for this task

What I used it for

I used Claude to run a parallel research sweep across ASX sub-$500M companies, scoring candidates on a crisis-comms and IR-failure rubric: sharpness of the retail impact, specificity of the failure, viability of the company, and demonstrability of the fix. BrainChip surfaced because its failure is a content and transparency choice — independently documented, quotable, and directly fixable — not just a generic "boring PDFs" problem.

What didn't work

Early research passes over-indexed on dramatic share price drops, which kept returning distressed shells where an IR fix is meaningless. I added a viability gate explicitly. Claude also initially conflated BRN's research partners with paying customers — the exact same mistake BrainChip's own deck makes — which I caught and corrected, and which sharpened the diagnosis.

Most useful workflow

Fan-out then adversarial verify: use AI to generate many candidates quickly, then use it to stress-test the diagnosis of the finalist. The most useful prompt forced a trade-off: "What's the single most specific, defensible, non-obvious IR failure here — and what evidence would collapse the argument?" That question cut through generic observations and surfaced the capital-raise contradiction as the sharpest proof point.
