Background

Who is BrainChip?

BrainChip Holdings (ASX: BRN) makes neuromorphic AI chips. Its Akida processor is designed for ultra-low-power edge AI inference. Partners include ARM, Mercedes-Benz, NASA and Nvidia. It's one of Australia's most retail-heavy tech stocks, with ~95% of its register held by retail investors. Market cap: ~A$341M.

The Diagnosis

What's broken

01

The deck is a marketing pitch, not an investor update

Every quarterly update leads with TAM charts ($163B, $84B) and a logo wall of partners. Revenue is not disclosed. Paying customers are never distinguished from research partners. There are no unit shipment numbers. Retail holders cannot answer the most basic question: is this business growing?

02

The capital raise contradiction

In November 2025, BrainChip raised A$35M from institutions at A$0.175, a 10.3% discount. Weeks later, retail shareholders were offered a Share Purchase Plan capped at A$2M at a near 52-week low. The SPP closed at A$2.976M. A US$25M raise followed in December. Retail got the worst pricing, the smallest allocation, and no explanation of how the structure was decided.

03

Shareholders said it out loud and nothing changed

At the 2025 AGM, shareholders voted 76.6% against the remuneration report, the third consecutive strike. Attendees named communication failures, executive pay, and share price performance as specific concerns. The company said nothing publicly. No town hall, no letter to shareholders, no acknowledgment that the vote had happened.

BrainChip's problem isn't silence. They publish constantly, to an audience that already knows the TAM story.

The Fix

What I'd do about it

One page. Every quarter. No excuses.

A Retail Shareholder Scorecard: one page, published every quarter alongside the ASX lodgement. Cash position, revenue if any, pipeline status, near-term catalysts. BrainChip already has this data. The question is what they put on the front page.

Process

AI Log

How I used AI for this task

What I used it for

I used Claude to scan ASX sub-$500M companies and score them on IR-failure criteria: how specific the failure was, how directly it affected retail holders, whether the company was viable, and whether the problem was actually fixable. BrainChip kept surfacing because the failure is a deliberate content choice, not an oversight. It's documented in public filings and there's a clear counter-argument to stress-test against.

What didn't work

Early passes kept pulling up companies where the share price had collapsed. An IR reframe doesn't help a distressed shell, so I added a viability filter. Claude also lumped BRN's research partners in with paying customers, which is the same conflation BrainChip's own deck makes. Catching that actually sharpened the diagnosis.

Most useful workflow

Fan-out then adversarial verify. Generate many candidates fast, then turn the AI around to stress-test the one you're leaning toward. The most useful prompt I ran was: "What's the single most specific, defensible IR failure here, and what evidence would collapse the argument?" That pushed past generic observations and surfaced the capital raise structure as the sharpest proof point.
